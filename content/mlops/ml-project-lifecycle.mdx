## What it is

MLOps applies engineering practices to building, shipping and operating machine learning systems.

## Why it exists

A notebook that works once is far from a dependable service. Data changes, models decay and pipelines break.

## Syntax and key APIs

Common maturity stages: manual notebooks, automated training pipelines, then CI/CD for pipelines with automated retraining.

## Simple example

```python
def promote(candidate: float, production: float, min_gain: float, checks_passed: bool) -> bool:
    return checks_passed and candidate >= production + min_gain

assert promote(0.84, 0.81, 0.01, True)
assert not promote(0.815, 0.81, 0.01, True)
assert not promote(0.9, 0.81, 0.01, False)
```

A promotion gate like this prevents an automated pipeline from releasing a worse model.

## Industry examples

Recommendation systems retrain frequently. Credit models change rarely, under strict governance and documentation.

## Patterns and best practices

- Establish a non-ML baseline and fallback.
- Automate only after a manual process works.
- Assign clear ownership for models in production.
- Keep a model registry with lineage.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

A batch job may be all that's needed. Managed platforms reduce effort and may lead to vendor lock-in.

## Common mistakes

- Treating deployment as the finish line.
- No plan for label collection.
- Training code that differs from serving code.
- No rollback path.
