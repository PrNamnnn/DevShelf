## What it is

- **Data drift:** input distributions change.
- **Concept drift:** the relationship between inputs and the target changes.
- **Prediction drift:** the output distribution changes.

## Why it exists

Models decay silently, and they may keep responding normally while their quality falls.

## How it works

Monitor four layers: service (latency, errors), data (nulls, schema, ranges, drift), model (prediction distribution), and business outcomes, which may arrive late.

Drift tests on large traffic flag trivial changes, so combine statistics with effect sizes and thresholds. The **Population Stability Index** is common, and values above 0.25 are often considered significant drift, a convention rather than a rule.

Retrain on a schedule or by trigger, and always evaluate against the current model first.

## Syntax and key APIs

<Formula>PSI = Σ (aᵢ − eᵢ) ln(aᵢ / eᵢ)</Formula>

Here eᵢ and aᵢ are the expected and actual bin shares.

## Simple example

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

def psi(expected, actual, bins=10):
    edges = np.percentile(expected, np.linspace(0, 100, bins + 1))
    edges[0], edges[-1] = -np.inf, np.inf
    e = np.histogram(expected, edges)[0] / len(expected) + 1e-6
    a = np.histogram(actual, edges)[0] / len(actual) + 1e-6
    return float(np.sum((a - e) * np.log(a / e)))

rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
train = rng.normal(0, 1, 10_000)
stable = rng.normal(0, 1, 10_000)
shifted = rng.normal(0.8, 1, 10_000)

assert psi(train, stable) < 0.05
assert psi(train, shifted) > 0.25
assert stats.ks_2samp(train, shifted).pvalue < 0.001
```

## Industry examples

Fraud models are monitored continuously because adversaries adapt, and demand models are retrained on regular cycles.

## Patterns and best practices

- Page on-call for service problems, and notify on slow drift.
- Monitor segments, not only totals.
- Log features and model version for each prediction.
- Validate retraining before promotion.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

Online learning adapts continuously but is riskier. Scheduled retraining is simple and may waste resources.

## Common mistakes

- Relying on drift alerts without business impact.
- Retraining on leaked or corrupted recent data.
- Ignoring feedback loops, in which predictions affect future labels.
- Not tracking delayed labels.
