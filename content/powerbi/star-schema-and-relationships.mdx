## What it is

A **star schema** has **fact tables** that record events and measures, surrounded by **dimension tables** that describe them. Relationships connect dimensions to facts.

## Why it exists

Power BI's engine and DAX are designed for this shape. It produces smaller models, simpler measures and predictable filtering. A single wide table can cause repeated values, slower calculations and awkward filtering.

## How it works

Filters flow from the "one" side to the "many" side. Selecting a product in `DimProduct` filters `FactSales`.

| Cardinality | Use |
| --- | --- |
| One-to-many | The normal case |
| One-to-one | Rare, often better merged |
| Many-to-many | Advanced, and sometimes needs a bridge table |

Duplicates on the "one" side make a refresh fail. Bi-directional filtering should be used only when genuinely needed. Relationships don't enforce referential integrity, so unmatched facts appear under a blank dimension member.

Role-playing dimensions, such as order date and ship date, use one active and additional inactive relationships activated through `USERELATIONSHIP`, or separate copies.

## Syntax and key APIs

```text
DimDate[Date]        1 -> *  FactSales[OrderDate]
DimProduct[Key]      1 -> *  FactSales[ProductKey]
DimCustomer[Key]     1 -> *  FactSales[CustomerKey]
```

## Simple example

Instead of a flat table with repeated product names, keep one row per product in `DimProduct` and only `ProductKey` in the fact. Declaring a measure on the fact gives correct totals for whichever attribute slices the report.

## Industry examples

Retail, finance and logistics all use star models, often sharing conformed date and product dimensions across multiple fact tables.

## Patterns and best practices

- Define the grain of every fact first.
- Mark a Date table and hide foreign keys.
- Avoid bi-directional relationships.
- Prefer a single-table dimension over deep snowflakes.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

Snowflake schemas save a little storage but add joins. A single flat table is fine for simple, one-off reports.

## Common mistakes

- Relationships built between two fact tables.
- Duplicate keys in a dimension.
- Bi-directional relationships that cause ambiguity.
- Mixed grains in one fact table.
