## What it is

Probability is a number from 0 to 1 that expresses how likely an event is. **Conditional probability** P(A | B) is the probability of A given that B occurred. Events are **independent** when knowing one doesn't change the probability of the other.

**Bayes' theorem** reverses a conditional, updating a prior belief in light of evidence.

## Why it exists

Statistics rests on probability, and human intuition is notably poor at it. People confuse P(alert | fraud) with P(fraud | alert), which can be very different numbers.

## How it works

Core rules:

| Rule | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Complement | P(not A) = 1 − P(A) |
| Addition | P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B) |
| Conditional | P(A given B) = P(A and B) ÷ P(B) |
| Multiplication | P(A and B) = P(A given B) · P(B) |
| Independent | P(A and B) = P(A) · P(B) |

Mutually exclusive isn't the same as independent. If two events can't both occur and each has positive probability, then learning one happened tells you the other didn't, so they're dependent.

## Intuition and formula

<Formula>P(H | E) = P(E | H) · P(H) / P(E)</Formula>

P(H) is the prior. P(E | H) is the likelihood. P(E) is the total probability of the evidence, and the result is the posterior.

## Worked example

2% of transactions are fraudulent. A detector flags 90% of fraud and 5% of legitimate transactions. Out of 10,000 transactions, 200 are fraud, of which 180 get flagged. Of the 9,800 legitimate ones, 490 get flagged. So P(fraud | flag) = 180 ÷ 670 = 26.9%. Most alerts are false alarms despite an apparently accurate detector, and that's the base-rate effect.

## Python

```python
fraud = 0.02
flag_given_fraud = 0.90
flag_given_legit = 0.05

flag = flag_given_fraud * fraud + flag_given_legit * (1 - fraud)
fraud_given_flag = flag_given_fraud * fraud / flag
assert round(fraud_given_flag, 3) == 0.269

# Independence check on a contingency count.
total, a, b, both = 1_000, 300, 200, 60
assert both / total == (a / total) * (b / total)
```

## SQL

```sql
SELECT round(avg(fraud) FILTER (WHERE flagged = 1), 3) AS p_fraud_given_flag
FROM (
  SELECT 1 AS fraud, 1 AS flagged FROM generate_series(1, 180)
  UNION ALL SELECT 1, 0 FROM generate_series(1, 20)
  UNION ALL SELECT 0, 1 FROM generate_series(1, 490)
  UNION ALL SELECT 0, 0 FROM generate_series(1, 9310)
) AS t;
-- rows: [{"p_fraud_given_flag":"0.269"}]
```

## In industry

Conditional rates drive funnels ("given they added to cart, did they buy?"). Bayes' theorem underlies spam filters and A/B platforms that report the probability a variant is best.

## Assumptions and limitations

Bayesian results depend on the prior, and probabilities estimated from history assume stable conditions. Independence is frequently assumed for convenience: sessions from one user aren't independent.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing P(A | B) with P(B | A).
- Ignoring base rates.
- Treating exclusive events as independent.
- Multiplying probabilities of correlated failures.

## Alternatives

Natural frequencies, such as "180 of 670", communicate conditional probability more clearly than formulas. Simulation answers probability questions that are hard to derive analytically.

## Interview questions

<Question q="A test is 99% accurate. Does a positive result mean a 99% chance of disease?">No. It depends on prevalence. With a rare disease, most positives can be false.</Question>

<Question q="Can mutually exclusive events be independent?">Only if at least one has probability zero. Otherwise one occurring rules out the other, which is dependence.</Question>
