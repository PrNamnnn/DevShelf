## What it is

Exploratory data analysis, a term popularized by John Tukey, is open-minded investigation of a dataset before formal modelling. It's how you learn what the data contains, what's broken and what's worth testing.

## Why it exists

Skipping exploration leads to polished results on flawed data. EDA catches join explosions, mislabeled units and missing periods, and it often produces the actual insight.

Exploration and confirmation need to stay separate. Patterns you noticed after trying dozens of cuts are hypotheses. Test them on fresh data or in an experiment.

## Worked example

A retail analyst loads a year of orders. Grain: `order_id` should be unique, but there are 1.2% duplicates from a retry bug. Profiling shows November is missing two days. Quality checks find negative amounts, which turn out to be refunds that belong in another analysis. A time-series plot then shows a weekly cycle, and segmenting finds that mobile average order value fell after an August redesign. That final observation becomes a hypothesis for a controlled test.

## Python

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

orders = pd.DataFrame({
    "order_id": [1, 2, 2, 3, 4, 5],
    "device": ["web", "mobile", "mobile", "web", None, "mobile"],
    "amount": [120.0, 80.0, 80.0, -40.0, 150.0, np.nan],
})

profile = pd.DataFrame({
    "missing_pct": orders.isna().mean().round(3) * 100,
    "unique": orders.nunique(),
})
duplicates = orders.duplicated("order_id").sum()
invalid = (orders["amount"] < 0).sum()

assert duplicates == 1
assert invalid == 1
assert profile.loc["device", "missing_pct"] == 16.7
```

## SQL

```sql
SELECT count(*) AS row_count,
       count(DISTINCT order_id) AS distinct_orders,
       count(*) - count(amount) AS missing_amounts,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE amount < 0) AS negative_amounts
FROM (VALUES (1, 120.0), (2, 80.0), (2, 80.0), (3, -40.0), (5, NULL))
       AS o(order_id, amount);
-- rows: [{"row_count":5,"distinct_orders":4,"missing_amounts":1,"negative_amounts":1}]
```

## In industry

A short EDA note saved with a project lets others reuse the discoveries. Teams also turn repeated findings into automated data-quality checks.

## Assumptions and limitations

EDA finds patterns, including false ones. The more cuts you examine, the more that appear by chance. Any finding is conditional on the data's coverage and quality.

## Common mistakes

- Skipping the grain check.
- Treating an EDA finding as proven.
- Only looking at averages.
- Forgetting to analyze time, the single most common source of hidden problems.

## Alternatives

Automated profilers speed up the first pass, and their output can be saved. A pre-registered analysis, which fixes the plan in advance, is the right mode for confirming rather than exploring.

## Interview questions

<Question q="What are your first steps with an unfamiliar dataset?">Establish the grain and the key, check date coverage, profile quality, then explore distributions and relationships with the outcome.</Question>

<Question q="How do you avoid false discoveries during EDA?">Keep exploratory findings labelled as hypotheses, count how many cuts were tried, and confirm on held-out data or through an experiment.</Question>
