## What it is

**Growth accounting** divides change in users or revenue into flows: new, expansion, resurrected, contraction and churned. **Revenue decomposition** separates a change into price, volume and sometimes mix.

## Why it exists

A headline growth rate can hide a leaky business. Decomposition assigns the change to something a team owns.

## How it works

The **SaaS quick ratio** is (new + expansion) ÷ (contraction + churn). Above 1, inflows exceed outflows. **Net revenue retention** uses only existing customers.

**Price-volume** analysis uses the convention below. The two effects sum to the total change, but other attribution conventions distribute interaction differently, so state which one you used.

## Intuition and formula

<Formula>end = start + new + expansion − contraction − churn</Formula>

<Formula>volume effect = (Q₁ − Q₀) · P₀, price effect = (P₁ − P₀) · Q₁</Formula>

## Worked example

Starting MRR is ₹100,000, with new ₹8,000, expansion ₹3,000, contraction ₹2,000 and churn ₹5,000. Ending MRR is ₹104,000, a 4% rise. Quick ratio is 1.57, and net revenue retention is 96%: existing customers shrank, and new sales hid it.

A product moves from 1,000 units at ₹100 to 950 units at ₹105. Volume effect is −₹5,000, price effect is +₹4,750, and the total is −₹250.

## Python

```python
start, new, expansion, contraction, churn = 100_000, 8_000, 3_000, 2_000, 5_000
end = start + new + expansion - contraction - churn
assert end == 104_000
assert round((new + expansion) / (contraction + churn), 2) == 1.57
assert (start + expansion - contraction - churn) / start == 0.96

p0, q0, p1, q1 = 100, 1_000, 105, 950
volume, price = (q1 - q0) * p0, (p1 - p0) * q1
assert (volume, price) == (-5_000, 4_750)
assert volume + price == p1 * q1 - p0 * q0
```

## SQL

```sql
WITH m(customer, prev_mrr, cur_mrr) AS (
  VALUES ('a', 100, 100), ('b', 100, 150), ('c', 100, 60), ('d', 100, 0), ('e', 0, 80)
)
SELECT sum(cur_mrr) FILTER (WHERE prev_mrr = 0) AS new_mrr,
       sum(cur_mrr - prev_mrr) FILTER (WHERE prev_mrr > 0 AND cur_mrr > prev_mrr) AS expansion,
       sum(prev_mrr - cur_mrr) FILTER (WHERE cur_mrr > 0 AND cur_mrr < prev_mrr) AS contraction,
       sum(prev_mrr) FILTER (WHERE cur_mrr = 0) AS churned
FROM m;
-- rows: [{"new_mrr":80,"expansion":50,"contraction":40,"churned":100}]
```

## In industry

Used constantly in board reports, SaaS finance and pricing reviews.

## Assumptions and limitations

Decomposition is arithmetic, not causation. Mix effects need item-level data. Quick ratios can mislead at small scale, where one customer has a large impact.

## Common mistakes

- Double-counting reactivations.
- Mixing currency effects with price.
- Omitting the interaction convention.
- Using quick ratio without looking at absolute size.

## Alternatives

Shapley-based attribution fairly distributes changes among many factors, and regression-based decompositions estimate drivers.

## Interview questions

<Question q="What does a quick ratio of 1.5 mean?">For every rupee of revenue lost, 1.5 rupees of new or expansion revenue were added.</Question>

<Question q="Can revenue increase while volume falls?">Yes, if the price effect or a favourable mix shift outweighs the volume loss.</Question>
