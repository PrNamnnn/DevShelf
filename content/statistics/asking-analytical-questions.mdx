## What it is

An analytical question names something measurable about a defined group over a defined period, compared with a defined baseline, in service of a decision. "How is the app doing?" isn't one. "Did 7-day retention of Android users acquired in March fall below February's cohort, and should we roll back release 8.2?" is one.

## Why it exists

The most expensive analytical error is answering the wrong question correctly. Vague requests also let results be read however a stakeholder prefers. Writing the question down forces agreement on what evidence would count before anyone sees the answer.

## How it works

Fill in six parts:

| Part | Prompt | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decision | What will change based on this? | Whether to roll back release 8.2 |
| Metric | What exactly is measured? | Users active on day 7 ÷ users who installed |
| Population | Who is included? | New Android installs, excluding test accounts |
| Window | Over what time? | Install cohorts from 1 to 31 March |
| Comparison | Against what? | February cohorts |
| Threshold | What result triggers action? | A fall of at least 2 percentage points |

Classify the question next, because the type determines the method: descriptive (what happened), diagnostic (why), predictive (what will happen), or prescriptive (what should we do).

## Worked example

A marketing lead asks, "Are our campaigns working?" After a short conversation it becomes: "For paid channels in Q3, which had a customer acquisition cost above ₹1,200 for customers who made a second purchase within 90 days, so we can reassign next quarter's budget?" This reveals a data need that the original request hid: acquisition cost must be joined to repeat purchases, and customers acquired late in the quarter need a full 90 days before they can be judged.

## Python

```python
from dataclasses import dataclass

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class AnalysisBrief:
    decision: str
    metric: str
    population: str
    window: str
    comparison: str
    threshold: str

    def is_complete(self) -> bool:
        return all(value.strip() for value in vars(self).values())

brief = AnalysisBrief(
    decision="Roll back release 8.2?",
    metric="Day-7 retention",
    population="New Android installs",
    window="March install cohorts",
    comparison="February install cohorts",
    threshold="Fall of 2 points or more",
)
assert brief.is_complete()
```

## In industry

Strong teams use a short written brief for non-trivial requests. It prevents repeated rework, and it records why a number was defined in a particular way when someone asks six months later.

## Assumptions and limitations

Stakeholders may not know the decision yet, and exploratory work is legitimate. Label it as such, and put a time limit on it. A threshold set in advance is also a judgement call that needs business input rather than statistics alone.

## Common mistakes

- Accepting the requested chart without asking what it's for.
- Choosing a metric because it's easy to query.
- Changing the definition after seeing results.
- Comparing unequal periods, such as a 31-day month with a 28-day month, using totals.

## Alternatives

Hypothesis-driven framing begins with a suspected cause and looks for disconfirming evidence, which suits diagnostic work. Metric trees begin with one outcome and decompose it, which suits recurring reviews. Open exploration suits a new dataset, as long as findings are treated as hypotheses.

## Interview questions
