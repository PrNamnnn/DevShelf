## What it is

These three distributions describe test statistics under the null hypothesis. They're built from normal variables.

| Distribution | Built from | Used by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student's t | Normal ÷ √(chi-square ÷ df) | t-tests, regression coefficients |
| Chi-square | Sum of k squared standard normals | Count tables, variance tests |
| F | Ratio of two chi-squares, each ÷ df | ANOVA, regression F-test |

## Why it exists

When the population SD is unknown, replacing it with a sample estimate adds uncertainty. William Gosset derived the t-distribution, publishing under the pseudonym "Student", to account for exactly this with small samples.

## How it works

<DistributionExplorer only={["t", "chi2", "f"]} title="Sampling distributions" />

**Degrees of freedom** count the independent pieces of information left after estimating parameters. With df small, t has heavy tails. As df grows it approaches the standard normal. Chi-square and F are non-negative and right-skewed, and they're typically tested using the upper tail.

Relationships worth remembering: the square of a t variable with v df follows F(1, v), and chi-square with 1 df is a squared standard normal.

## Intuition and formula

<Formula>t = Z / √(χ²ᵥ / v)</Formula>

<Formula>F = (χ²_a / a) / (χ²_b / b)</Formula>

## Worked example

The two-sided 5% critical values vary with df: normal 1.96, t with 29 df 2.045, t with 9 df 2.262. Small samples require more evidence. A chi-square critical value is 3.84 for df = 1 and 7.81 for df = 3. In a one-way ANOVA with 3 groups and 15 observations, the 5% F critical value with (2, 12) df is 3.89.

## Python

```python
from scipy import stats

assert round(stats.t.ppf(0.975, 9), 3) == 2.262
assert round(stats.t.ppf(0.975, 29), 3) == 2.045
assert round(stats.chi2.ppf(0.95, 1), 2) == 3.84
assert round(stats.chi2.ppf(0.95, 3), 2) == 7.81
assert round(stats.f.ppf(0.95, 2, 12), 2) == 3.89

# t squared equals F(1, v) at the matching tail.
assert round(stats.t.ppf(0.975, 12) ** 2, 6) == round(stats.f.ppf(0.95, 1, 12), 6)
```

## Excel or Power BI

`T.INV.2T(0.05, 9)`, `CHISQ.INV.RT(0.05, 1)` and `F.INV.RT(0.05, 2, 12)` return critical values. `T.DIST.2T`, `CHISQ.DIST.RT` and `F.DIST.RT` return p-values.

## In industry

You rarely consult these directly, since software reports them, but they explain why small experiments need bigger effects and why regressions report t and F statistics.

## Assumptions and limitations

Exact results depend on normal errors and independence. Larger samples relax normality through the Central Limit Theorem, though not independence.

## Common mistakes

- Using the wrong degrees of freedom.
- Assuming t and z differ meaningfully at large sample sizes.
- Applying a two-sided value to a one-sided question.
- Treating chi-square's upper-tail test as directional.

## Alternatives

Permutation tests and the bootstrap build the reference distribution directly from the data.
