## What it is

- **AOV**: revenue ÷ orders.
- **ARPU**: revenue ÷ users.
- **CAC**: acquisition spend ÷ new customers.
- **CLV (or LTV)**: the expected margin from a customer over the relationship.
- **Payback**: months needed to recover CAC from margin.

## Why it exists

Revenue alone doesn't reveal whether growth is profitable. Unit economics connect marketing, retention and pricing.

## How it works

Use contribution margin, not revenue, in CLV. Calculate CLV by acquisition cohort and channel. Include all relevant costs in fully loaded CAC.

An LTV:CAC ratio of 3 is often quoted as healthy. It's a rule of thumb, and it doesn't replace looking at payback and cash.

## Intuition and formula

For retention r per period, margin m per period, discount rate d, with the first payment arriving after one period:

<Formula>CLV = m · r / (1 + d − r)</Formula>

<Formula>CAC payback = CAC / monthly margin per customer</Formula>

## Worked example

Monthly margin is ₹40 and retention is 0.8. Without discounting, CLV = 40 × 0.8 ÷ 0.2 = ₹160. With 1% monthly discounting it's ₹152.38.

₹120,000 of spend acquired 400 customers, so CAC is ₹300. With ₹35 monthly margin, payback is 8.6 months. ₹250,000 from 1,000 orders gives an AOV of ₹250.

## Python

```python
margin, retention = 40, 0.8
clv = margin * retention / (1 - retention)
discounted = margin * retention / (1 + 0.01 - retention)
assert round(clv, 2) == 160
assert round(discounted, 2) == 152.38

cac = 120_000 / 400
assert cac == 300
assert round(cac / 35, 1) == 8.6
assert 250_000 / 1_000 == 250
```

## SQL

```sql
SELECT channel,
       round(spend / customers, 2) AS cac
FROM (VALUES ('search', 60000.0, 300), ('social', 60000.0, 100)) AS t(channel, spend, customers)
ORDER BY cac;
-- rows: [{"channel":"search","cac":"200.00"},{"channel":"social","cac":"600.00"}]
```

## Excel or Power BI

Use `NPV` for discounted cash flows, and a data table to show CLV sensitivity to retention and margin. In DAX, CAC is a `DIVIDE` of spend and distinct new customers.

## In industry

Investors and finance teams use cohort CLV, and marketing budgets are allocated by channel payback. Short payback protects cash in tight periods.

## Assumptions and limitations

Constant retention overstates or understates true value. Young cohorts need projections. Attributing CAC across channels is itself uncertain.

## Common mistakes

- Using revenue instead of margin.
- Blended CAC hiding an unprofitable channel.
- Using a long-run CLV for a young business.
- Averaging AOV across segments without weights.

## Alternatives

Survival or BG/NBD models estimate purchasing for non-contractual businesses, and uplift models value the incremental effect of marketing.

## Interview questions

<Question q="Why is margin better than revenue in CLV?">Revenue ignores the cost of delivering value, and can make an unprofitable customer look valuable.</Question>

<Question q="What's wrong with blended CAC?">It averages paid and organic customers, hiding the actual cost of the marginal customer.</Question>
