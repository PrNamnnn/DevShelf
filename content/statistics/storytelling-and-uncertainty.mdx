## What it is

Data storytelling structures analysis so that a specific audience understands what happened, why it matters and what to do.

## Why it exists

An analysis that isn't understood or acted upon had no value. Decision makers need a conclusion, and they need to know how sure you are.

## How it works

Lead with the answer. Use this pattern:

1. **Situation:** what were we trying to learn?
2. **Finding:** the main claim in one sentence.
3. **Evidence:** the one or two charts that prove it.
4. **Uncertainty:** the range and what could make it wrong.
5. **Recommendation:** the action, owner and date.

Title each chart with its takeaway instead of a topic. Express uncertainty with intervals, and use language consistently. Separate what the data shows from your interpretation.

Report what you didn't find too, along with analyses that were inconclusive.

## Worked example

Weak: "Here is a chart of churn by plan." Strong: "Monthly-plan churn doubled after the billing change, so reverting it could retain an estimated 900 to 1,400 customers a quarter. We recommend reverting in the next release, and validating with a holdback." The range is the honest part, and the holdback addresses remaining uncertainty.

## Python

```python
import io
import matplotlib

matplotlib.use("Agg")
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

labels = ["Control", "Treatment"]
rates = [16.0, 17.6]
margins = [0.51, 0.53]

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(5, 3))
ax.errorbar(labels, rates, yerr=margins, fmt="o", capsize=4, color="#2f5fc9")
ax.set_ylabel("Activation (%)")
ax.set_title("Guided onboarding lifted activation by about 1.6 points")

buffer = io.BytesIO()
fig.savefig(buffer, format="png")
plt.close(fig)
assert buffer.tell() > 1_000
```

## Excel or Power BI

Add error bars with custom values in Excel. Power BI's error bars and smart narrative visual can add uncertainty and generated summaries, which should still be reviewed.

## In industry

Written decision documents are common for important decisions, with appendices that hold the methodology.

## Assumptions and limitations

A compelling story can oversimplify. Don't hide contradictory evidence, and adapt the depth to the audience without changing the conclusion.

## Common mistakes

- Walking through every step in the order you did it.
- Titling charts with only a topic.
- Giving a point estimate to look confident.
- Making no recommendation.

## Alternatives

Interactive reports suit exploration by analysts, and one-page memos suit executives.
