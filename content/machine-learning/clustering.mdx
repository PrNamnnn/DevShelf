## What it is

Clustering groups similar observations without labels.

| Method | Finds | Needs k | Handles noise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| K-means | Spherical clusters | Yes | No |
| DBSCAN | Dense, arbitrary shapes | No, but needs eps | Yes |
| Gaussian mixture | Elliptical, soft assignment | Yes | Limited |

## Why it exists

Customer segmentation, grouping documents and anomaly discovery all happen without ready-made labels.

## How it works

**K-means** alternates between assigning points to their nearest centroid and recomputing centroids, minimizing within-cluster squared distance. **DBSCAN** grows clusters from points with enough neighbours within eps. **Gaussian mixtures** are fitted by **expectation-maximization**: the E-step calculates each point's responsibility, and the M-step updates means, covariances and weights.

Use silhouette scores, stability checks and business usefulness to evaluate results.

## Worked example

On two interleaved half-moons, K-means slices straight across the structure, and DBSCAN recovers both moons. On blobs of differing elongation, a Gaussian mixture does better than K-means.

## Python

```python
from sklearn.cluster import DBSCAN, KMeans
from sklearn.datasets import make_blobs, make_moons
from sklearn.metrics import adjusted_rand_score, silhouette_score
from sklearn.mixture import GaussianMixture

X, y = make_moons(n_samples=500, noise=0.05, random_state=0)
kmeans = KMeans(2, n_init=10, random_state=0).fit_predict(X)
dbscan = DBSCAN(eps=0.2, min_samples=5).fit_predict(X)
assert adjusted_rand_score(y, dbscan) > 0.95 > adjusted_rand_score(y, kmeans)

blobs, _ = make_blobs(n_samples=300, centers=[[0, 0], [6, 6], [0, 6]], cluster_std=0.6, random_state=0)
scores = {k: silhouette_score(blobs, KMeans(k, n_init=10, random_state=0).fit_predict(blobs)) for k in [2, 3, 4]}
assert max(scores, key=scores.get) == 3

gmm = GaussianMixture(3, random_state=0).fit(blobs)
assert gmm.predict_proba(blobs).shape == (300, 3)
```

## In industry

K-means is common for segmentation, due to its speed. DBSCAN is often used for spatial and anomaly tasks.

## Assumptions and limitations

Clustering always returns something, including from random data. Results depend on scaling and on the distance measure.

## Common mistakes

- Skipping scaling.
- Using the elbow plot as the sole criterion.
- Running K-means on one-hot data without care.
- Treating cluster labels as stable identifiers across runs.

## Alternatives

Hierarchical clustering provides a dendrogram, and HDBSCAN handles varying densities.

## Interview questions

<Question q="When is K-means a poor choice?">With non-spherical shapes, very different cluster sizes or densities, or heavy outliers.</Question>

<Question q="What does EM do?">It alternates between soft-assigning points and updating cluster parameters to raise the likelihood.</Question>
